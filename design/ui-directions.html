FieldGuide, two ways forward

A review of the current mobile UI, then two design directions to choose between. Nothing is built into the app yet. Use the switch to preview each in light and dark.

What I found in the current app

Works well

  • Study mode already has a bottom dock with a 46–50px primary button.
  • Swipe left and right moves between cards in Read mode.
  • Bottom sheets for menus and settings suit one-handed use.

Too busy

  • Home stacks a stats card, search, grouping toggle, folder button, folder chips, a section heading and folder headers before the first deck.
  • Each deck card repeats its folder as a pill, plus a menu, progress bar and three mode buttons.
  • The study header has a back button, title, settings, three-way mode switch, progress bar and filter chips above the card.

Touch and gestures

  • Measured on a 390px phone: the header icon buttons are 38px and the mode switcher is 32px tall. Both are under the 44px minimum.
  • Per-deck mode buttons are 32px tall. Many text sizes are 11–12px.
  • Swipe works only in Read. Recall and Quiz have no gesture. No dark/light option; the app is dark only.
  • Emoji double as icons, which render differently on each device.
Direction A

Quiet Gallery

Photo first, almost no chrome. The species is the hero and everything else steps back. Calm, closer to a printed field guide than a game.

Daily Trainer

A practice habit first. The home screen answers "what should I study today", and study mode uses large cards you throw left or right. More energy, chunkier shapes.

Shared by both directions

Light, dark, or systemThree-way setting, remembered. Defaults to your phone's setting.
44px minimum targetsIcon buttons, mode switch and rows. Primary actions are 54–64px.
Swipe in every modeSideways to move or grade. Down on the study sheet to close, up for more.
Fewer things on screenSearch, folders and filters move behind one control. Deck actions use long-press or a sheet.
Drawn icons, not emojiOne consistent icon set, so it looks the same on every phone.
Reduced motion respectedCard flips and slides are skipped when the device asks for it.

Which one?

A, Quiet Gallery if you mostly browse and look at the photos. It is the calmer of the two and the closest to your current layout, so it is the smaller change.

B, Daily Trainer if you want to drill and build a habit. It changes the home screen and study flow more, and the swipe-to-grade gesture is the main new idea.

You can also mix them, for example A's study screens with B's home. Tell me which, plus anything you would change, and I will build it into the app.
